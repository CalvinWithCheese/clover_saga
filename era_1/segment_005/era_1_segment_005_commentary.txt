Commentary

This segment does a lot of world-positioning for Clover. Dragonsreach is not just a civic building to her; it becomes a test of whether power has earned its symbols. Her fixation on the dragon skull over Balgruuf is perfect because Clover reads status through deeds. A throne is less persuasive to her than the question of who earned the bones above it.

Her political bluntness also feels exactly right. Asking Balgruuf whose side he is on before she has any courtly context is Clover in full forward motion. She wants clarity, loyalty, backbone, and a direct answer. Balgruuf's duty-shaped ambiguity may be wise rulecraft, but Clover evaluates it like a warrior looking for a battle line.

The Farengar scene is funny because Clover's pride and anti-magic suspicion collide with an insult she only partially understands. Smashing the alchemical salts is childish, physical, immediate, and very Clover. She may not win the exchange intellectually, so she wins it by making the table suffer.

Amren's sword gives the segment its heart. Clover's response to the lost heirloom shows that her idea of glory is not only self-display. Honor matters to her in a direct and embodied way. A father's sword belongs with the family, thieves should not have it, and a deed worthy of the Companions is suddenly right there in front of her.

The herbalism turn at the end is a nice grounding detail. After dragons, politics, court wizards, and sworn family blades, Clover still has practical knowledge from the plains. She clunks into the apothecary, but she is not empty-handed. She brings rough local competence with her.
