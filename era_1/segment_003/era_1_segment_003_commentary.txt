Commentary

This segment gives Clover her first real taste of the life she has been imagining, and it wisely refuses to make the fight clean or effortless. She is strong, brave, and effective, but she is also winded, shot, impulsive, and repeatedly balancing instinct against survival. That mixture is exactly where Clover is most alive as a character.

The gate sequence is particularly good. Clover does not simply sprint into the courtyard at first; she uses cover, bellows a challenge, and forces the first bandit to come to her. Then, once the first kill feeds her spirit, tactics start losing the wrestling match against glory. That is a great Clover rhythm: she is capable of sense, but excitement keeps trying to bite through the leash.

Erik matters here in a way that strengthens both of them. He is not just background support. His movement on the ramparts and later injury affect Clover's choices, and Clover's rush to check on him after the fort is cleared makes the friendship feel lived-in and sincere.

The aftermath is also strong. Dragging bodies together, stripping usable gear, upgrading armor, and hauling spoils back under the full moon gives the victory texture. Clover's fulfillment is not abstract. It is sore muscles, loot weight, battlefield pride, and the sense that she has finally stepped into the story she wanted.
